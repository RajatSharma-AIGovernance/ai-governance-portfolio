# 2.3 - Singapore Model AI Governance Framework (GenAI Edition)

This handbook serves as an enterprise operational reference for navigating AI compliance within Singapore. It translates the **IMDA Model AI Governance Framework for Generative AI** into clear enterprise check-points, matching regional requirements with plain-English definitions, corporate risks, and practical deployment fixes.

---

## 🏛️ Module 1: The Singapore AI Governance Landscape

### The IMDA & PDPC Framework
Singapore approaches AI governance through a highly systemic, innovation-friendly, yet strict risk-management lens led by the **Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA)** and the **Personal Data Protection Commission (PDPC)**. Rather than issuing blanket bans, Singapore launched its updated **Model AI Governance Framework for Generative AI** to specifically tackle modern LLM risks like hallucinations, copyright infringement, and data leakage.

### The Role in AI Governance
For senior technology professionals looking at APAC hubs, Singapore’s framework is the gold standard for pragmatic AI testing. It introduces **AI Verify**, an open-source testing toolkit that allows enterprises to run objective technical audits on fairness, explainability, and robustness. Satisfying Singapore's governance expectations is a major asset when building out scalable systems for multi-national financial institutions operating across Southeast Asia.

## 🔍 Module 3: Deep-Dive Operational Breakdown (The 3 Core Strands)

To pass a regulatory review under the IMDA guidelines, an enterprise deployment must actively implement three core operational controls:

### 1. Trusted Sourcing & Content Provenance
* **What the Regulation Means:** Ensure transparency regarding data inputs and implement mechanisms to distinguish between human-generated and machine-generated content.
* **In Simple Terms:** Clearly label AI-generated content so users aren't deceived, and carefully track where your training datasets came from.
* **The Real-World Fix:** Implement automated metadata tagging across all AI outputs and ensure all third-party vendor training data lines carry verified cryptographic certificates.

### 2. Independent Model Auditing (AI Verify Integration)
* **What the Regulation Means:** Utilize open and standardized testing frameworks to validate model claims regarding bias, fairness, and safety.
* **In Simple Terms:** Don't just take a developer's word for it that the AI is safe. You must run standardized data tests to prove it.
* **The Real-World Fix:** Mandate that before steering committee approval, the AI Governance Lead must execute an independent bias evaluation suite against protected demographic datasets.

### 3. Third-Party GenAI Supply Chain Accountability
* **What the Regulation Means:** Delineate clear responsibilities between model developers (foundation model builders) and model deployers (enterprises customizing the model via RAG).
* **In Simple Terms:** When you build a RAG app using an external model (like OpenAI or AWS Bedrock), you must formally outline what risks the vendor controls versus what risks *your* company controls.
* **The Real-World Fix:** Execute an internal **Risk Split Assessment Matrix** with the procurement legal team before deploying any vendor API hook, ensuring your data inputs are never utilized for external foundation model training.
